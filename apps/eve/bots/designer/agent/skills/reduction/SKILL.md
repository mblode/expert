# Reduction

## 1. See it as it is

Open the real screen in the browser on my screen and screenshot it. Not
the design file, not the description: the thing people use, at a desktop
width and at a phone width, signed in and signed out.

## 2. Name its job

One sentence: what is this screen for, and what does the person do next.
Write it down. Everything after this is measured against it.

## 3. Inventory

List every element on the screen. For each one, one of three verdicts:

- **Does the job.** Keep.
- **Helps someone do the job.** Keep, but it can probably be smaller,
  later, or behind something.
- **Does neither.** Remove. Decoration, a second call to action, a
  helpful tip nobody reads, a badge, a border, an icon beside a word that
  already says it.

Count what you removed. That number is the work.

## 4. Rebuild in order

Words, then hierarchy, then space, then colour. Colour last, always. Most
screens that feel wrong are a labelling problem with a spacing problem on
top, and neither needs a new palette.

## 5. The states

Draw the empty, loading, error, and too-much states of what is left. A
design that only exists in its happy state is half a design and the
missing half is the half people meet on their first day.

## 6. Hand it over

A prototype in one HTML file under `/workspace/design/<product>/`, a
screenshot of before and after in the message, and a short list: what was
removed, what changed, and what it cost. Then a handoff file for Software
Engineer with the states and the behaviour spelled out.
