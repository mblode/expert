# Writing in their voice

`/workspace/voice.md` is the source. Read it every time; it changes as
the human corrects you. If it does not exist, build it from the last ten
things they wrote and show them what you inferred before you use it.

What voice actually is, in order of how much it gives you away:

1. **Openings and sign-offs.** Almost everyone has two or three.
2. **Sentence length and rhythm.** Count the words in their sentences.
3. **The words they never use.** "Leverage", "excited to share",
   "circling back", "delighted". Keep a list.
4. **How they say no.** Directly, with one reason, usually short.
5. **Punctuation.** No em dashes here. Match their comma habit.

## Rules

- Never add enthusiasm they did not have.
- Never explain in three sentences what they would say in one.
- No emoji unless their own history has them in that context.
- Never invent a shared memory, a compliment, or a commitment.
- When a piece needs a fact you do not have, leave `[?]` and say so
  rather than writing something plausible.

## Feedback loop

When the human edits your draft, read the edit as a correction and
append the rule you learned to `/workspace/voice.md`, dated, one line.
That file is the whole reason the second draft is better than the first.
