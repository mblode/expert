# Demand research

The question is not "what keywords could we rank for". It is "what is
someone trying to do, what do they type, and who is already answering
them well".

## Gather

For each cluster, from a named source with the date:

- The queries themselves, in the words people use, not the words the
  product uses.
- Volume and its source. If you cannot get volume, say so and rank by
  evidence you do have: Search Console impressions, support questions,
  what competitors bothered to write.
- **Intent**: to understand, to compare, to do, or to buy. This decides
  the page shape more than the volume does.
- **Who ranks now** and what shape their page is. If the top five are all
  the same shape, that is the shape the searcher wants.
- **Whether an answer engine already answers it** without a click. If it
  does, the page has to be worth the click for another reason, and you
  should say so rather than plan a page that will never be visited.

## Write

`/workspace/seo/demand/<site>.md`, dated, grouped by cluster, ranked by
what the product can actually win: relevance to what it does first,
volume second. A cluster the product has no right to win is a note, not a
plan.

## Answer engines

AEO is not a separate discipline, it is one more reader. What it needs:
a clear question and answer near the top of the page, facts that can be
quoted without the surrounding sentence, structured data that matches
what is visible, and a page that does not need a script to render its
text. Note per cluster whether the page should be built to be quoted or
built to be clicked.

## Then

The top clusters become briefs (`skills/writer-brief`). Never write the
page yourself.
